SlideFit Glove: A Low-Cost, Kinematically Compatible, and Size-Adaptive Glove for Dexterous Manipulation

Anonymous Authors

Code Design PCB

Abstract

Teleoperation gloves collect high-quality demonstrations for dexterous manipulation by recording human hand kinematics and retargeting them to robotic hands with high degrees of freedom (DoF). However, existing exoskeleton gloves require complex calibration to estimate joint angles through nonlinear mappings, and their bulky design impedes natural motion while failing to accommodate diverse hand morphologies. To address these limitations, we present SlideFit Glove, a kinematically compatible glove with size adaptability. Its sliding-slot mechanism combines translation and rotation to track finger joint motion, decoupling angle sensing from length adjustment to convert nonlinear mapping errors into linear offsets. The slot's redundant travel also accommodates variations in finger size and wearing position, eliminating the need for repeated calibration. The SlideFit Glove independently measures 21 human joint angles and provides vibratory feedback at five fingertips, weighing approximately 290 g. Experiments show that over an 11 mm finger length range, the sliding-slot mechanism reduces average angular mapping error by 4.5° compared to fixed-linkage, with consistent accuracy across hand morphologies. Driving three robotic hands with 10–20 DoF across three real-world tasks, SlideFit Glove teleoperation is 8.6 s faster and achieves a 28% higher success rate than kinesthetic teaching, while direct human-centric data collection further reduces completion time by 6.5 s relative to teleoperation, offering pretraining data for imitation learning to accelerate policy convergence. At a total cost of approximately $69.4, SlideFit Glove constitutes a practical path for low-cost, large-scale dexterous manipulation data collection.
